Each number is the holding's typical move, in percent, when that one factor moves by the amount in the column heading and the others stay flat. How this works, and what it leaves out

Past crises. For each window the program takes every holding's adjusted closing price (dividends and coupons included) on the first and last trading day, and every day in between. Today's weights are applied as if the portfolio had been bought on the first day and left alone.

What-if shocks. A regression of each holding's weekly return on weekly moves in the S&P 500, the 10-year Treasury yield, oil, the dollar and gold gives its sensitivities. A scenario's result is the sum of sensitivity times shock across the factors.

Limits to keep in mind. The model is a straight line: it assumes an 8% fall is simply eight times a 1% fall, and ignores bond convexity and options. Sensitivities and correlations come from one period and change over time; in 2022 stocks and bonds fell together, which did not happen in 2008. The replay uses the yields and valuations of the past, not today's; a bond fund bought today at a different yield would earn different income through the same crisis. Everything is in US dollars, so currency risk for a Hong Kong dollar or other investor is not shown. Only assets are stressed, not liabilities or cash needs. These results are for learning and discussion, not investment advice.
